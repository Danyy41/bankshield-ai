# POL-AML-001 — Transaction Monitoring & SAR Filing Policy

**Document type:** AML policy
**Version:** 4.2
**Effective date:** 2026-01-05
**Owner:** BankShield Financial Crime Compliance (FCC)
**Applies to:** All retail and business deposit accounts

## §1. Purpose and scope

This policy governs how BankShield monitors transactions for money-laundering
typologies, how analysts document findings, and when a Suspicious Activity
Report (SAR) must be filed with FinCEN. It applies to all alerts generated by
the transaction-monitoring system, including alerts escalated from the
fraud-detection and cyber-authentication pipelines described in
POL-FRAUD-002 and POL-ATO-003.

## §2. Risk-scoring thresholds

### §2.1 Alert tiers

BankShield's model produces a continuous fraud/AML risk score in [0, 1].
Alerts are triaged into three tiers:

- **Tier 1 (score ≥ 0.80):** High risk. Requires review by a Level 2 analyst
  within 4 business hours. Consequential actions (account restriction, case
  creation naming a customer) require documented human approval per
  POL-CASE-004 §3.
- **Tier 2 (0.50 ≤ score < 0.80):** Elevated risk. Requires review within 1
  business day.
- **Tier 3 (score < 0.50):** Monitored. Reviewed in weekly batch unless
  correlated with a Tier 1/2 alert on a linked account (see §4).

### §2.2 Structuring indicators

Transactions just under reporting thresholds, repeated across a short window,
are a structuring indicator regardless of the model score. Three or more
cash-equivalent transactions (`cash_withdrawal`, `wire_transfer`) in 24 hours
that individually fall under $10,000 but sum above it must be escalated to
Tier 1 even if the model score is lower, per FinCEN structuring guidance.

## §3. High-risk merchant categories

`crypto_exchange`, `gambling`, and `wire_transfer` categories carry elevated
inherent AML risk. A Tier 2+ alert on a transaction in one of these
categories must include, in the case file:

1. The customer's account age and prior transaction history in that category.
2. Whether the transaction is cross-border (`country_mismatch = true`).
3. Whether the beneficiary or counterparty is newly established
   (`new_beneficiary = true`).

## §4. Network and mule-ring indicators

### §4.1 Shared-infrastructure signal

Analysts must treat `graph_shared_device_count`, `graph_shared_ip_count`, and
`graph_beneficiary_connectivity` above zero as evidence the transaction touches
shared infrastructure also used by other customers. This alone is not
sufficient for a SAR, but combined with `graph_suspicious_neighbor_count` > 0
(i.e., a network neighbor has a prior confirmed fraud transaction) it meets
the threshold for Tier 1 escalation regardless of the standalone risk score.

### §4.2 Ring characteristics

Historically, confirmed mule rings at BankShield exhibit: 3–7 linked
accounts, a shared pool of devices/IPs/beneficiary IDs, and a fraud rate
roughly 8–10x the population baseline among ring members. See
POL-GRAPH-006 for the full typology reference.

## §5. SAR filing criteria

A SAR must be filed when **all** of the following hold:

1. The alert reaches Tier 1 (§2.1) or meets the structuring criteria (§2.2).
2. A human reviewer has documented the specific evidence supporting a
   suspicion of money laundering, fraud, or another reportable activity.
3. The case has been approved per the human-approval workflow in
   POL-CASE-004 §3 — **no SAR-relevant case may be created or filed without
   explicit analyst approval.**

SARs must be filed within 30 calendar days of the initial detection of
facts that may constitute a basis for filing, per 31 CFR 1020.320.

## §6. Documentation standard

Every case file supporting a SAR decision (or a decision *not* to file) must
cite the specific evidence considered: transaction ID(s), the model's risk
score and top contributing features, relevant cyber-authentication signals,
and any network/graph signals, per §4. Conclusions unsupported by cited
evidence in the case file are not acceptable for audit purposes.
